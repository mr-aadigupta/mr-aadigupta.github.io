Web Dev Hackathon

Flowgrid (ADG Hackgrid)

View Source Code Visit Live Site

What it is: Flowgrid is a smart, voice-activated assistant built to help busy hospitals manage their resources without the chaos of endless phone calls and paperwork.

The Problem

In emergency situations, hospital staff waste precious time searching databases or calling around to find out if a bed, a specific specialist (like a cardiologist), or a piece of equipment is currently available. We wanted to solve this operational nightmare.

How It Works

Instead of clicking through complicated menus, a nurse or doctor simply speaks into the system. For example, they can say: "Emergency. We need a cardiologist, a bed in the CICU, and an ECG machine."

  • Speech Recognition: The system captures the voice and translates it into editable text using an AI model (faster-whisper).
  • Confirmation: The staff reviews the text on the screen to ensure the AI heard them correctly, keeping humans in control.
  • Automated Search: Once confirmed, the system's "Multi-Agent" backend acts like several digital assistants working at once. One checks bed availability, another finds the doctor, and another locates the equipment.
  • The Result: It instantly pairs up a complete, compatible set of resources. If the hospital is out of ECG machines, the system will explicitly tell the staff exactly what is missing so they can plan accordingly.

Development Background

We built this prototype during the ADG Hackgrid hackathon. After initially planning a finance project, we pivoted to healthcare. I helped develop the Python and Streamlit backend, integrating the speech AI and building a simulated hospital database to prove the concept works. Our goal was to create a Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) tool that hospitals could easily adopt to save time and lives.

Hardware Embedded Systems Hackathon

ESP32 Aviation GPWS & Auto-Flap Landing System

Our team, Fantastic Four, secured the second position (as freshers) in the Sensored BeyondX hackathon organized by the TEC club at VIT Vellore.

1. The Problem: Planes Crashing into the Ground

Sometimes, perfectly good airplanes crash into the ground or water simply because the pilot gets confused in bad weather, fog, or the dark. This is called CFIT (Controlled Flight Into Terrain). Standard plane sensors (barometers) can get confused by weather changes and aren't accurate enough when the plane is just feet away from hitting the ground.

2. Our Solution: A Smart Safety Net

We built a hardware system using an ESP32 microchip that acts as an automatic co-pilot to stop these crashes. It uses two ways to measure height and tilt to keep the plane safe:

  • Double-Checking Height: We combined a standard pressure sensor (for flying high) with an ultrasonic radar (like a bat uses) to measure the exact distance to the ground with pinpoint accuracy when the plane gets low.
  • Watching the Tilt: An accelerometer constantly monitors the angle of the plane to see if it's nose-diving dangerously.
  • Automatic Life-Saving Action: If the system realizes the plane is diving too fast or too steeply near the ground, it doesn't just warn the pilot—it acts. It instantly flashes red lights, sounds a loud emergency siren, and automatically forces the wing flaps into a safe position (0°) to help prevent a crash.
  • Smooth Landing Assist: If the plane is coming in at a safe angle, the system automatically adjusts the flaps (to 45° then 90°) to help it land smoothly, taking work off the pilot's plate.
